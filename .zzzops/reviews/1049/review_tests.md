# G1049 test review, attempt 6

The new local-remote tests cover the main owner-approved boundary: an in-band workspace claim while recovery is paused, competing recoveries, detection of an already changed work ref, and disclosure of a direct push. A focused run of the claim case fails at the missing recovery diagnostic (5.191 s), before reaching any race barrier, so neither the test harness nor the claimed fencing behavior has yet been exercised. The test writer measured the four-test red run at 21.813 s and the full module pair at 87.561 s; local process/remote costs are reasonable for this safety boundary, though green timings remain unknown.

Two material gaps prevent approval:

1. The competing-recovery test uses `time.sleep(0.5)` and does not establish that its contender actually reached the gate while the first recovery was paused. Both recoveries could execute sequentially and the existing goal CAS would still make exactly one succeed. Replace timing with a deterministic contender-ready barrier at the claim/push boundary, assert the contested overlap, and demonstrate that a change to the gate protocol makes the test fail. Do not require the gate ref to live outside all `zzzops/lease/` paths: the approved design specifies an actually updated CAS ref, not a particular namespace.
2. The approved design explicitly requires crash/expiry/reclaim and coverage of every work-ref mutating or workspace-claiming engine entry point. The suite tests only `start` while recovery owns the gate, not a previously claimed publisher, sync/rebase/publish paths, or a holder whose gate expires and is reclaimed. Add the smallest practical real Git/CLI cases and parameterize where useful: at minimum a stale holder must be unable to publish after reclamation, a crashed gate must be recoverable, and a pre-existing publisher or sync must be fenced against recovery. Include the remaining entry-point audit in reviewable evidence; enforce refusal where a path cannot participate.
