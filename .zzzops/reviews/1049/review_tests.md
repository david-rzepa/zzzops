# G1049 test re-review

The revised checkpoint adds meaningful real Git tests for two blocked attempts, recovery proposals, historical evidence, stale CAS and live leases. The writer measured both modules at 45.198 s red; that cost is material but justified by real repository/CLI journeys and the per-path protection requirement. I independently ran the enabled-capability retry and 13-path diagnosis cases (6.687 s), which both fail on the old engine: the former stops at capability rejection, and the latter at missing `scope_recovery`. These are useful contract checks but the red results do not yet prove their later assertions execute.

Both earlier findings are partially resolved. The remaining essential cases are:

- The enabled journey never exercises the specifically approved sync/rebase mapping, fail-closed unverifiable provenance, candidate exclusion/review of source/gitlinks, or a real consumer submission with an already-published implementation tip while its producer is blocked. The last case is currently only a direct helper call, which cannot catch an unguarded submit path. Add focused real Git/CLI coverage for the critical boundaries, and exercise old writers against the enabled capability.
- The recovery fixture's `partial_done` copies `self.accepted` and changes its frozen list and change hash, but leaves the exact same base and checkpoint commit. It therefore does not model G12's later, distinct 2-path checkpoint nor demonstrate recovery keeps a later real checkpoint unchanged and invalidates reviews correctly. Add a later real checkpoint with the 2-path subset, verify its identity before/after CAS and retry/review, and cover ambiguous ancestry or edited history as a no-proposal case. Existing missing-object, stale-base, concurrent-lease, unrelated-path, and done-state tests are valuable.
