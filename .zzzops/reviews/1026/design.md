## 1. Outcome

The owner is interviewed whenever a design depends on a choice that belongs to the owner, and is not bothered about anything else. Approvals stay delegated. The loop uses parts the engine already has:

1. A reviewer that finds an owner's choice raises a material finding whose text starts `owner question:`, giving the question, the options and a recommendation.
2. The finding reopens the design. The designer does not decide the choice. It submits `--blocked` (`waiting_for: human`) with the question. The existing blocked human item reaches the root.
3. The root batches owner questions across goals and asks the owner in one sitting. It records the owner's words with `answer`. The retry card's `answer` brings them back.
4. The revised design records the owner's words verbatim as owner decisions. The finding is not on the retry card (the design's record was blocked, so it is no longer live there), so the designer gives no response for it. It stays open on the review's record. The review re-runs and resolves it only when the engine-recorded answer, which `human_decisions` shows on the review card, covers it and the design quotes that answer.
5. `approve_design`, `approve_merge` and the PR body list every answered question, read from goal history, with the answer and who gave it. A delegated approval therefore still shows what the owner decided.

A design with no owner questions costs no extra runs.

The test for an owner's choice is written once, as a new POLICY instruction (`owner_questions`). The prompts that need it cite it.

## 2. The test (instruction `owner_questions`, shipped text)

> **Owner questions.** A choice belongs to the owner when undoing it, if it turned out wrong, would reach outside this goal's own change. That covers: other goals that build on it; users' repositories or installed policy; stored records or formats; names or outputs that others consume, such as CLI output, branch names and config keys; and external systems. A choice that changes what the product does for its users, or the project's direction, also belongs to the owner. The exception is when one option clearly dominates. Every other choice belongs to the designer, who decides it and records the reasoning; a reviewer who disagrees raises an ordinary finding. **Reviewers:** for each owner's choice the work makes, or leaves open, raise a finding on the design (`target`: the design node) whose text starts `owner question:` (that prefix is reserved for this; never use it for anything else), with the question, the options and your recommendation. It is always material. Do not ask again about a choice the design records as an owner decision; if the design contradicts one, raise an ordinary material finding. Resolve an owner question only when an entry in `human_decisions` on your card answers it and the design's `## Owner decisions` section quotes that answer; otherwise keep it open. **Designers:** never decide an owner's choice. Ask it with `--blocked`, as your prompt says.

This text follows the owner's wording: undo reach replaces "goal-sized", and choices with big consequences are asked. It is about 1,200 bytes, so a card shows it as a blob view (`INLINE` is 600). A project may tighten or loosen the test by editing its own POLICY, as it can with any instruction.

Listing it on a node does require the key, so the template's POLICY and workflow change together.
- **In-flight goals:** no record goes stale: instructions and prompts are not fingerprinted. The next review round of each goal applies the test.
- **Rollback:** a policy branch removes the instruction and the prompt lines. The engine change is read-only, so reverting it loses nothing.

## 8. Interaction with G1028

- **Findings across edits (G1028 item 1):** if the spec is edited while an owner question is open, G1028 keeps the finding live on the next design card, so the designer still blocks on it. G1026 does not depend on this. Without G1028, the root relays the question, as it does for other findings today.
- **`follows` (G1028 item 3):** a partly answered owner question becomes a new `owner question:` finding with `follows: <id>`. Nothing in G1026 reads `follows`. The answers in D4 are attached to the blocked record, not to a finding, so they need no link.
- **Resolved findings (G1028 item 2):** D4 does not rely on resolved findings being retained; it reads answers from goal history.
- **Stale cards:** G1028 leaves the stale-card answer to G1026, and D5 covers it. G1028 also carries material findings, so `owner question:` ones, across stale reviews. A spec edit made while `design` is blocked and not yet answered turns the blocked item into a `stale` design card. That card does not list the owner-question correction, with or without G1028, because a blocked record's findings are not live. The question is not lost: the finding stays open on `review_design`'s record. When the designer submits from the stale card, `review_design` re-runs, sees the finding under `previous.findings`, keeps it open (the design records no owner decision for it), and it reopens `design` as a correction, so step 4 blocks again. That costs one extra design and review run, only in this rare case. The root may save it by relaying the open question to the designer. G1026 adds no stored key, so it has no capability coordination with G1028.

## 9. Risks

- **Over-asking.** Reviewers raise owner questions for details. Mitigations: the test's "clearly dominates" exception and its undo-reach rule; the instruction tells reviewers not to ask again about recorded decisions; a designer who thinks a choice is its own can answer `disputed: …`, which escalates to the human as a dispute after the usual rounds. The threshold lives in project text, so it can be tuned without engine work.
- **Under-asking.** Reviewers miss an owner's choice. The Intent lens already looks for "decisions taken on the owner's behalf" and now raises them, and `adversary_design` carries the instruction as well.
- **The delegate answers in the owner's place.** This is the failure that prompted the spec. The skill forbids it, and every approval item and the PR body show `decided_by` for each answer, so it is visible.
- **Misquoting or a wrong mapping.** The displayed quote is read from the `answer` commit, not from the designer's text. The re-review sees the same entries on its card and resolves an owner question only when an entry answers it and the design quotes it. A designer that maps an answer to the wrong finding id, or records a decision the owner never gave, is therefore caught at review, not only at approval. What remains is reviewer error. The approve prompts also show `human_decisions` next to the design.
- **Unattended runs** stall the goal at the blocked item. That is intended. stage_first scheduling and safe useful work keep the other goals moving.
- **Card size.** The instruction is shown as a blob view. `human_decisions` is not clipped on cards; `shrink` blobs the whole list as one view (§5). Review cards also carry `previous.findings`, which no step shrinks, so a review card can still end above the 4,096-byte target, as it can today; the new list never adds more than one view reference (about 150 bytes) once blobbed.
- **History cost and availability.** One `git log` per goal per render, plus one `git show` per answer commit. Answers are few: one per human round. The goal refs are fetched with history, which `card.changed` already relies on. If the log fails, `render.human_decisions` returns `None`, and the approve item and the card carry the string value `"human_decisions": "unavailable"` instead of a list. The PR body's section reads "Unavailable: goal history could not be read." This avoids showing an empty list as if nothing had been decided.
- **Commit subject as an index.** Renaming the `answer` subject would break the read. SPEC §3.9 makes the form part of the contract, and test 3 pins it.
